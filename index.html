About

I research AI in and for organizations: how AI can raise productivity while preserving and fostering human expertise.

I am a PhD candidate in Information Systems at the Karlsruhe Institute of Technology (KIT), in the Digital Service Innovation group at the Institute for Information Systems and KSRI, advised by Prof. Gerhard Satzger. I examine how AI shapes human decision-making and reliance, and how incentives and work design can make productivity and expertise reinforce each other.

My background is quantitative. I studied Industrial Engineering and Management (B.Sc.) and Information Systems (M.Sc.) at KIT, with exchange semesters in Kaunas and Klagenfurt, and worked on machine learning and technology strategy at TRUUCO, Siemens and Netze BW before starting my PhD. Today my work bridges human–computer interaction, computer science and management science.

From theory to practice

I work on the same question at several levels, from formal models to systems that people use at work:

  • Formal modeling. Models of how skills build up through practice and erode through reliance on AI, and of the incentives that keep people learning.
  • Measuring LLMs. Metrics for how reliable the models people depend on are, for example how much their answers change with the wording of a prompt.
  • Building with industry. LLM-based systems designed with companies in construction and public procurement, evaluated on real documents and with domain experts.

I also teach in the master's course Artificial Intelligence in Service Systems at KIT and have supervised 14 bachelor's and master's theses on LLM agents, RAG and uncertainty quantification.
