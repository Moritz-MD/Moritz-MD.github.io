Hi there, I’m Moritz!

I’m a PhD candidate in Information Systems at the Karlsruhe Institute of Technology (KIT), advised by Gerhard Satzger in the Digital Service Innovation group. I research how organizations can use AI to become more productive while helping people develop and retain their expertise.

My work moves between theory and practice. I build formal models of how skills grow and erode when people work with AI, evaluate the LLMs people rely on, and design and test LLM-based systems together with companies in construction and public procurement. Before my PhD, I studied Industrial Engineering and Management (B.Sc.) and Information Systems (M.Sc.) at KIT and worked on machine learning and technology strategy at TRUUCO, Siemens and Netze BW.

Outside work, I train for triathlons, play classical guitar, and spend as much time as I can in the mountains.

Research focus

Skill erosion and incentives in AI-assisted work

Relying on AI saves effort but can take away the practice people need to build and keep their skills. In our model, novices can stay at low skill and experts can lose skill even while AI-assisted accuracy stays high. We characterize the payments for correct independent work that help people acquire and retain skills. Building on this, I am modeling skill hierarchies: when delegating a foundational task to AI erodes the higher-order skills built on it, and which task assignments or incentives prevent this.

2026 preprint, under review at ACM FAccT 2027 · arXiv

Reliability of the LLMs people rely on

How much do LLM answers change when people rephrase the same question? We test how prompt wording and specificity affect answer accuracy, and propose a metric that separates the effect of wording from the variation you get by running the same prompt repeatedly.

Under review at ICLR 2027 · OpenReview submission

LLM-based systems in organizations

With a small business, we built an LLM-based system for public tender screening. Across 34 tender documents, initial screening took 59% less time than manual screening, at 88.8% accuracy for text fields and 83.9% for yes/no checks. In think-aloud sessions with five experts, we observed a shift from extracting information to checking the system’s outputs. In the NaiS project, we built ConSight for retrieving information from large collections of construction documents.
