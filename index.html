I am a PhD candidate in Information Systems at the Karlsruhe Institute of Technology (KIT), in the Digital Service Innovation group at the Institute for Information Systems and KSRI, advised by Prof. Gerhard Satzger.

I research AI in and for organizations, asking how AI can raise productivity while preserving and fostering human expertise. I examine how AI shapes human decision-making and reliance, and how incentives and work design can make productivity and expertise reinforce each other.

My work bridges human–computer interaction, computer science and management science. I combine practical experience developing and evaluating LLM-based systems in industry projects with formal modeling, mechanism design and design-oriented research.

Research approach

I like to work on the same question at several levels of abstraction, from formal models to systems that people use at work:

  • Formal modeling. Mathematical models of how skills build up through practice and erode through reliance on AI, and of the incentives that keep people learning (FAccT 2027, under review).
  • Measuring LLM behaviour. Metrics for how reliable the models people depend on actually are, for example how much their answers change with the wording of a prompt (ICLR 2027, under review).
  • Building systems with industry partners. Design science projects with companies: interviews to understand the work, LLM-based prototypes, and evaluations on real documents and with domain experts (ECIS 2026, WI 2025).
