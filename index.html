About

When AI does more of the work, what happens to human expertise, and how can organizations keep people learning?

I am a PhD candidate in Information Systems at the Karlsruhe Institute of Technology (KIT), in the Digital Service Innovation group at the Institute for Information Systems and KSRI, advised by Prof. Gerhard Satzger. I research AI in and for organizations: how AI can raise productivity while preserving and fostering human expertise. My work focuses on three themes:

  • Skill erosion and reliance. How expertise builds through practice and wears down when people rely on AI.
  • Incentives and work design. How organizations can make productivity and expertise reinforce each other.
  • Reliable LLMs. How dependable the models people rely on actually are.

I study these questions with formal models, LLM evaluations, and systems built and tested with industry partners (see my research). Before my PhD, I studied Industrial Engineering and Management (B.Sc.) and Information Systems (M.Sc.) at KIT, with exchange semesters in Kaunas and Klagenfurt, and worked on machine learning and technology strategy at TRUUCO, Siemens and Netze BW.
