Skill erosion and incentives in AI-assisted work

2026 preprint · under review at ACM FAccT 2027

In a model where workers gain skill through independent practice, novices can remain at low skill and experts can lose skill even while AI-assisted accuracy stays high. We characterize the payments for correct independent work that help workers acquire and retain skills, and show when independent work becomes self-sustaining without further incentives.

Scaffolding skill hierarchies in AI-assisted work

Work in progress

Higher-order skills rest on foundational tasks that AI now takes over. I am building a formal model of skill hierarchies to ask:

  • When does delegating a foundational skill to AI erode the higher-order skills built on it?
  • Which task assignments or incentives preserve opportunities for independent practice while meeting productivity goals?

Measuring prompt sensitivity

Under review at ICLR 2027

We test how prompt wording and specificity affect answer accuracy, and propose a metric that separates the effect of wording from the variation you get by running the same prompt repeatedly.

LLM-based systems for complex knowledge work

ECIS 2026 · selected for AIS Insights

With a small business, we built an LLM-based system for public tender screening. Across 34 tender documents, initial screening took 59% less time than manual screening. Accuracy was 88.8% for text fields and 83.9% for yes/no checks. In separate think-aloud sessions with five experts, we observed a shift from extracting information to checking the system’s outputs.
